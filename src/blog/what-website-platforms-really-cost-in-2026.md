Every platform quotes you the number that makes them look cheapest. The real cost lives in what&rsquo;s missing from that number &mdash; hosting, apps, transaction fees, the seat you didn&rsquo;t know you&rsquo;d need. Below is what small businesses are actually paying in 2026, platform by platform, with a comparison table for each.

Prices reflect each platform&rsquo;s standard published rate, generally the discounted rate for annual billing where one is offered. Month-to-month billing typically runs 25 to 40 percent higher unless noted.

## Self-Hosted WordPress

Shared hosting looks cheap up front, but that&rsquo;s an introductory rate that jumps sharply at renewal. Managed WordPress hosting is the realistic baseline for a small business: reliable infrastructure, automatic updates, and support that can actually help when something breaks.

| Hosting Tier | Monthly | Annual | Add-ons &amp; Extra Costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shared Hosting | $12&ndash;18 (renewal rate) | $150&ndash;215 | Theme, security &amp; backup plugins |
| Managed WordPress Hosting | $20&ndash;30 | $240&ndash;360 | Theme ($50&ndash;100 one-time), security &amp; backup plugins |

A well-run small business WordPress site lands at $300 to $800 a year all-in, before any custom design or development work.

### WooCommerce

WooCommerce itself is free, but Shopify&rsquo;s price bundles things WooCommerce sells separately: hosting, SSL, a CDN, PCI-compliant checkout, shipping labels, tax calculation, and abandoned cart recovery. The honest WooCommerce total has to include all of it.

| Component | Monthly | Annual | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WooCommerce-Optimized Hosting | $11&ndash;40 | $132&ndash;480 | Scales with traffic |

| Shipping Labels &amp; Live Rates | ~$8&ndash;10 | $100&ndash;120 | Free tier covers basics; live rates cost extra |
| Automated Multi-State Tax | ~$17+ | $200+ | Needed for sellers with multi-state nexus |

| Backups | ~$6 | $70 | UpdraftPlus Premium or similar |
| **Full Plugin Stack (feature parity with Shopify)** | &mdash; | **$800&ndash;2,000** | Combined total of the extensions above |

### The Real Comparison

At small scale, Shopify is faster to launch and the all-in cost is predictable: platform fee, processing, a handful of apps. WooCommerce takes more setup work but is typically cheaper once it&rsquo;s running. Past $500K in annual revenue, the gap widens to roughly $4,800 to $9,600 a year on Shopify versus $2,400 to $5,400 a year on WooCommerce including hosting. The trade is control and cost against convenience and speed &mdash; neither answer is wrong, but the sticker price on either platform is never the real number.

## Bottom Line

The advertised price is a starting point, not a budget. Hosting renewals, transaction fees, required seats, and the plugins or apps needed to match built-in features can double or triple what a platform looks like it costs on the pricing page. Budget for the whole stack, not the headline number.
